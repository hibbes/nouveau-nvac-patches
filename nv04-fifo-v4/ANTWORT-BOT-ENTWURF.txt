Taking the findings across the three replies together, since two of
them are the same point seen from 2/3 and 3/3.

The publication of chan->fence is the real one, and the bot is right.
The backends do

	fctx = chan->fence = kzalloc_obj(*fctx);

with a plain store, so a reader on another CPU can observe the pointer
before the allocator's zeroing stores are visible to it. The acquire on
fctx->ready in nouveau_channel_kill() orders everything after that load,
but it cannot order what came before the pointer itself, so on a
weakly ordered machine ->ready could be read as garbage and the handler
could take the uninitialised lock. x86 does not reorder stores, which is
why nothing shows here, but the argument in the commit message assumed
the zeroing was covered and it is not.

This is pre-existing: before 2/3 the same plain publish fed a
nouveau_fence_context_kill() that checked nothing at all. 2/3 narrows it
to the zeroing, it does not close it. Two ways to close it, and I will
take one for v5 once the review of the rest has come in:

  - publish with smp_store_release() in the five backends, so the acquire
    on ->ready also covers the allocation, or
  - hand over through a pointer that nouveau_fence_context_arm() sets
    itself, so there is no window in which the pointer is visible and the
    object behind it is not built.

The second is cleaner and touches less of the backends; I lean towards
it but would rather hear whether Lyude has a preference, given 2/3 is
her design.

The error path in nouveau_channel_init() I believe is covered, though
for a different reason than the flag. nv84_fence_context_del() clears
chan->fence before nouveau_fence_context_free(), and
nouveau_fence_context_del() runs synchronize_rcu() in between, so the
pointer is gone before the memory is. nouveau_channel_kill() cannot
reach a freed context through it.
